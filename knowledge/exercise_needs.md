# Exercise and Enrichment Needs

Guidance for scheduling walks, play, and enrichment. Every rule has a stable ID so a
generated plan can cite the rule it relied on.

## EX-001: Adult dogs need at least 30-60 minutes of activity daily
Most adult dogs need 30-60 minutes of walking or active play per day, usually split
across two sessions. High-energy working breeds need considerably more.

## EX-002: High-energy breeds need 90+ minutes and mental work
Border Collies, Australian Shepherds, Belgian Malinois, Huskies, and similar working
breeds need 90 minutes or more of activity, and physical exercise alone is not enough —
schedule training or puzzle-feeder sessions as separate enrichment tasks.

## EX-003: Golden Retrievers, Labradors, and similar sporting breeds need 60-90 minutes
Sporting retrievers are moderately high energy. Two walks plus a play or fetch session
is a good daily shape. Under-exercised retrievers develop destructive chewing.

## EX-004: Brachycephalic breeds must avoid heat and hard exercise
Pugs, Bulldogs, Boxers, Boston Terriers, Persian and Himalayan cats cannot cool
themselves efficiently. Schedule walks in the early morning or after sunset, keep
sessions short (15-20 minutes), and never in the heat of the day.

## EX-005: Senior pets need shorter, more frequent, low-impact sessions
Pets over roughly 8 years old do better with three short 15-minute walks than one long
one. Avoid stairs, jumping, and hard running for arthritic pets.

## EX-006: Puppies follow the five-minute-per-month rule
Structured exercise for a growing puppy should be about 5 minutes per month of age, up
to twice a day. A 4-month-old puppy gets roughly 20 minutes twice daily. Over-exercising
a puppy damages developing joints.

## EX-007: Cats need short, frequent play sessions
Two to three 10-15 minute interactive play sessions daily (wand toys, chase games) meet
most adult cats' needs. Cats are crepuscular, so sessions near dawn and dusk fit their
natural rhythm best.

## EX-008: The last walk of the day should come before the evening wind-down
A final short walk or litter-box check in the evening reduces overnight accidents.
Schedule it after the evening meal has settled, respecting the meal-exercise gap in
FEED-004.

## EX-009: Do not schedule strenuous exercise immediately after a vet procedure
For 24-48 hours after vaccinations, dental work, or surgery, restrict activity to short
leashed toilet breaks. Flag any plan that puts a hard exercise task on a procedure day.
