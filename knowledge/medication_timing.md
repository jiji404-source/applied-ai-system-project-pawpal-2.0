# Medication Timing and Administration

Guidance for scheduling pet medications. Every rule has a stable ID so a generated
plan can cite the rule it relied on.

## MED-001: Twice-daily medications need ~12 hours between doses
Medications prescribed BID (twice daily) should be spaced as close to 12 hours apart
as the owner's schedule allows. Doses given less than 8 hours apart risk stacking blood
concentration; doses more than 16 hours apart leave a coverage gap. A common workable
pattern is 08:00 and 20:00.

## MED-002: Antihistamines and allergy pills are given with or after food
Oral antihistamines (including common allergy tablets) cause stomach upset on an empty
stomach. Schedule an allergy pill at the same time as a meal or within 30 minutes after
it — never 2 or more hours before the next meal.

## MED-003: Thyroid medication is given on an empty stomach
Levothyroxine and similar thyroid medications absorb poorly when given with food.
Schedule at least 1 hour before a meal, or 3 hours after one. This directly conflicts
with MED-002 style "give with food" rules, so a pet on both needs separated slots.

## MED-004: Do not schedule two oral medications in the same time slot
Unless a veterinarian has explicitly approved co-administration, two different oral
medications at the same clock time make it impossible to attribute a reaction to one
drug. Separate them by at least 1 hour.

## MED-005: Insulin is given immediately after a meal, twice daily
Insulin injections must follow food. Schedule the injection within 30 minutes AFTER the
pet has eaten, not before. If the pet refuses the meal, the dose is skipped and the vet
is contacted — never given on an empty stomach.

## MED-006: Flea, tick, and heartworm preventives are monthly, not daily
Topical and oral parasite preventives are dosed monthly. A daily or weekly recurrence on
a parasite preventive is a scheduling error and should be flagged. Pick a fixed calendar
day each month.

## MED-007: Missed-dose rule for once-daily medications
If a once-daily dose is more than 6 hours late, give it when remembered and shift the
next dose to restore the normal interval. Do not double up to catch up.

## MED-008: Pain medication (NSAIDs) is given with food
Veterinary NSAIDs (carprofen, meloxicam and similar) irritate the stomach lining when
given alone. Pair with a meal, and never schedule two different NSAIDs on the same day.
